Lab 5 · Spreadsheet guide

The starting worksheet supplies labels and units, not measurements or solved formulas. Work in this sheet as you would in Lab 3. Written lab-report sections belong in your separate report.

1 · Data and timing trials

Rows 3–9 are your pendulum lengths. Columns A–C contain length L in meters, its uncertainty δL, and the count N of complete oscillations timed per trial. D–H contain total elapsed times for up to five independent trials. Do not type the period into the total-time columns. At least three distinct lengths and two trials at each length are needed; five lengths and 3–5 trials are recommended.

2 · Writing spreadsheet formulas

Every formula begins with =. Refer to numbers by their cell addresses, such as A4, rather than retyping measurements. A colon indicates a continuous range of cells: C6:C10 includes C6 through C10. Do not type units or mathematical symbols such as ×, ÷, or superscript digits inside formulas.

The examples below show spreadsheet syntax only, using unrelated numbers and cells. They are not Lab 5 answers. You must determine which operations and references are appropriate for each quantity in your worksheet.

Fill down: enter a formula in one row, select that cell and the cells below it using Shift-click, then choose Fill down. Relative cell references change with the row. For example, an unrelated formula =A4/1000 becomes =A5/1000 when filled down one row.

Absolute references: a dollar sign locks a row or column when filling. $B$12 always refers to B12, while B12 changes relatively. You can combine fixed and changing references in one formula.

Functions ignore blank cells and text within ranges when appropriate; a blank cell referenced directly in arithmetic is different. Use only the functions listed here. Refer to the Lab 5 instructions for the physical definitions and calculations you need to implement.

3 · Weighted least-squares calculation

You can use Weighted fit to calculate a regression from your transformed data, or use the worksheet to compute the weighted sums and fitted parameters yourself. Check calculation compares your results with an independent fit without filling in your formulas.

With S=Σw, Sx=Σwx, Sy=Σwy, Sxx=Σwx², Sxy=Σwxy and Δ=S Sxx−Sx²:

B=(S Sxy−Sx Sy)/Δ
A=(Sxx Sy−Sx Sxy)/Δ
δB=√(S/Δ), δA=√(Sxx/Δ)
g=4π² exp(−2A), δg=2gδA

Use the labeled summary section for your calculations. The equations are provided in mathematical notation, not as cell-by-cell instructions. Fit uncertainties use the supplied vertical uncertainties and are not automatically scaled by √(reduced χ²).

4 · Graphs and fitting

Select Create graph, choose the columns containing your calculated horizontal values, vertical values, and uncertainties, and choose the data-row range. For the transformed pendulum plot, the axes are linear: you have already taken the natural logarithms of the measurements.

You may overlay a weighted fit calculated by the graphing tool or select Use A and B cells to plot a line based on fitted parameters in your worksheet. You can edit graph labels and axes after creation and download SVG or PNG figures. An optional residual graph can include a horizontal zero line.
